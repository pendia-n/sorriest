# The Apology Olympics

> Give an Oscar-worthy apology for something completely stupid.

## Concrete example

Apologise for eating your roommate's emergency cheese while performing as a corporate CEO addressing a scandal.

## User flow

1. A host buys a proposed **$2.99 match** and invites friends, who join free.
2. Each player gets a silly offence and a performance constraint or character.
3. The player records an apology: “Mistakes were made.”
4. The AI interviewer challenges the evasion: “You were alone with the cheese. Whose mistakes?”
5. The player records a follow-up response.
6. Friends vote **Forgiven** or **Make it worse**.
7. The app creates a mock press-conference highlight.

## Participation and AI roles

People perform, improvise their recovery, and vote. AI provides the fictional scenario, identifies an evasion, asks a contextual follow-up, and packages the highlight.

## Distinctive point

The humour comes from defending an increasingly bad apology, not generating an apology script. However, the underlying loop is the same as the courtroom and sales concepts: perform, get challenged, improvise.

## Smallest proposed version

Silly prompts, performance constraints, short recordings, one contextual interviewer question, voting, and a simple highlight. No need for a complex tournament system or generated video to test the joke.

## Competitive evidence and limits

The discussion did not establish an exact direct competitor. Improv games, party prompts, and ordinary group videos are substitute categories, not evidence of an identical commercial product. Absence of a discovered direct competitor is not proof of an open market.

## Risks — interpretation

- **Competition: High from substitutes.** Existing low-tech activities can satisfy much of the entertainment need.
- **Sherlocking: High.** A larger party game could add this as a mode or content pack.
- **Novelty:** Later rounds may recycle the same jokes after the initial amusement wears off.
- **Payment:** A funny prompt plus recording may not justify a purchase.
- **Social safety:** Never use the game to pressure someone into a real public apology.
- **Defensibility:** The theme is readily copied and the core interaction overlaps with the other proposed games.

## Recommendation

Treat this as a content pack, not a standalone product. It has not demonstrated a distinct business case or validated willingness to pay.

The intended model is a playful, participatory, one-off purchase rather than a subscription or a passive upload-and-wait AI generator. One buyer covers invited participants; invitations should not require another payment. Lead with the experience, but clearly disclose AI involvement, price, deliverable, recording handling, and failure/refund terms before checkout. Do not promise policies before implementing them.

Sherlocking means an established platform or adjacent product incorporates the central feature, reducing the reason to buy this standalone product. A distinctive experience is not automatically a durable moat.
